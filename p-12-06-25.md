Hello hello!

Thought I'd kickoff the exclusive Patreon with something exciting: I passed AI-900 on my first try! I have yet to fail a Microsoft Certified exam, which means I must be doing something right, so here's my BTS for how I passed, and how you can too.

## what even is AI-900

For those who don't know, Microsoft Credentials are industry-recognized certifications that demonstrate that you truly understand the tools you claim to know (and aren't just slapping them on your resume)

Employers treat them as proof you can walk into a relevant project without needing hand-holding. As you start stacking them, you can also get access to more specialized pathways/roles in the industry. Microsoft Partner organizations also receive benefits/recognition for having certified employees.

The certifications range from different topics across Azure Services to M365 products to Security and AI. The naming conventions, roughly, look something like this:

AZ for Azure, MS for Microsoft 365, SC for Security, etc. followed by numbers indicating product focus and expertise level (e.g., Fundamentals, Associate, Expert.)

- 900 for Fundamentals, 100s/200s for Associate, and 300s for Expert

AI-900 is the [Azure AI Fundamentals](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/certifications/azure-ai-fundamentals/?practice-assessment-type=certification&wt.mc_id=studentamb_516777) credential. It's described as covering "fundamental AI concepts related to the development of software and services of Microsoft Azure to create AI solutions."

## what you should study

AI-900 assesses on the following skills (with percentage of the exam on that topic):

- Describe Artificial Intelligence workloads and considerations **(15–20%)**

- Describe fundamental principles of machine learning on Azure **(15-20%)**

- Describe features of computer vision workloads on Azure **(15–20%)**

- Describe features of Natural Language Processing (NLP) workloads on Azure **(15–20%)**

- Describe features of generative AI workloads on Azure **(20–25%)**

In my experience, ML principles, computer vision, and NLP were the dominating questions I encountered.

**Make sure you know:**

- The differences between Classification, Clustering, and Regression (and when to apply them to different scenarios)

- The Responsible AI principles (fairness, reliability & safety, privacy & security, inclusiveness, transparency, accountability) and examples of them in action

- The features of each AI service available on Microsoft Foundry, primarily:

  - Azure OpenAI

  - Computer vision

  - Language service

  - Speech service

  - Document intelligence

  - Bot services

  - AI Search

## how to study properly

If you've been considering getting the credential, just book your exam now. Just do it. Go to [Pearson Vue](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/certifications/schedule-through-pearson-vue?examUid=exam.AI-900&examUrl=https://learn.microsoft.com/credentials/certifications&wt.mc_id=studentamb_516777), or [Certiport](https://certiport.pearsonvue.com/Certifications/Microsoft/Schedule) if you're a student/educator. You can book it a couple weeks out, which gives you a goal and a timeline to work towards. If you're anything like me, you'll probably procrastinate studying and taking the test until the end of time if you don't put it in your calendar now.

 I gave myself two weeks. I like having a strong technical background when I approach these sort of exams, so I took the [Azure AI Fundamentals AI-900 Exam Prep Specialization](https://www.coursera.org/specializations/microsoft-azure-ai-900-ai-fundamentals) and the [Microsoft AI & ML Engineering Professional Certificate](https://www.coursera.org/professional-certificates/microsoft-ai-and-ml-engineering) by Microsoft on Coursera. The former gives you everything you'll need to know for the exam, and the latter goes right down deep into it with a whole bunch of interactive practice activities and reflections on ML and Responsible AI, which will be a large part of the AI-900 exam. The professional certificate includes a lot outside the exam, so I would say it's optional as a study resource, but it's still fantastic for hands-on and visual learners to see different algorithms covered on the test in action which ended up being essential for me. It took me the full two weeks to complete those, working on them after my work day (roughly) between 5:00 - 9:00 PM.

Regularly visit the [Microsoft Foundry](https://portal.azure.com/?icid=azure-ai#view/Microsoft_Azure_ProjectOxford/CognitiveServicesHub/~/overview&wt.mc_id=studentamb_516777) for AI services and create resources! Most services can be tested and used on a **Free** (F0) tier, so there's no reason not to try them out. This is, in my opinion, the absolute best way to study for the workloads on Azure questions.

The day before/the morning of the exam is when you'll want to get that last-minute cram and refresh in. I highly recommend [John Savill's AI-900 Study Cram](https://youtu.be/bTkUTkXrqOQ?si=Oo611ZfpAOAtpzWY&wt.mc_id=studentamb_516777) (and the [Learning About Generative AI pt. 2](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ch6KE7KxHGM&wt.mc_id=studentamb_516777) video) as they're each only an hour long and cover/summarize everything pretty extensively.

Also TEST YOURSELF! The best way to get a feel for the exam format and be comfortable in your knowledge heading in is by using practice assessments. There are loads online, but many of them are locked behind a paywall. The best is to stick with [Microsoft's official practice assessment](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/certifications/exams/dummy/practice/assessment?assessmentId=26&assessment-type=practice&wt.mc_id=studentamb_516777), or take a look at the community-led [MSFT Hub Wiki](https://certs.msfthub.wiki/azure/ai-900/) for resources.
